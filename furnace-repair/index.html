A modern furnace runs a strict startup sequence, and knowing where the sequence breaks is most of the diagnosis. The thermostat calls, the inducer motor spins up to prove draft through a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter glows, the gas valve opens, the burners light, the flame sensor confirms fire, and only then does the blower deliver heat to the house. In Taunton, MA, the failures cluster at predictable points: an oxide-coated flame sensor, a hot surface igniter cracked from years of thermal cycling, a pressure switch that will not close because the inducer is weak or the condensate trap on a high-efficiency model has clogged, or a control board with a burned relay. Each one stops the sequence cold, and each one mimics the others from the hallway.

Our experienced technicians diagnose a furnace the way it operates, in order, with a meter at every step. That means igniter resistance, microamp flame signal, pressure switch operation under actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor condition. Safety checks are never optional on a gas appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak testing on the gas connections, and flue draft verification, since a hazard rarely shows itself at the thermostat. We finish by checking temperature rise against the data plate, making sure the burner and blower are matched the way the manufacturer designed them to.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we repair them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are already stocked on the truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. When to Call for Furnace Repair in Taunton

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Flames that start, last a few seconds, and go out again and again
  • Rust streaks, soot, or water around the cabinet or flue
  • A flame that burns soft and yellow rather than steady blue
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower
  • Short cycling every few minutes without reaching the setpoint
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • The furnace clicks and glows but the burners never light
  • Booming or banging at light-off from delayed ignition

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

The failure that matters most in a furnace is usually hidden. A cracked or corroded heat exchanger can let combustion gases into the airstream, and nothing about it is visible from the outside of the cabinet. Delayed ignition makes things worse, because each boom at light-off puts more stress on that metal. Add high voltage, a gas valve, and a flue to the same box, and the margin for error shrinks. Diagnosis is full of traps too. A high limit that keeps tripping could be a bad switch, or it could be doing exactly its job because a filter is clogged or the blower is dying, and replacing a good switch only hides the real fault.

At Taunton HVAC, no symptom gets treated in isolation. A meter tells us the flame signal, so we never condemn a good sensor while the real culprit, a cracked igniter or a weak gas valve, keeps causing trouble. We confirm draft at both the pressure switch and the flue, measure temperature rise against the data plate to flag airflow problems early, and leak-test every gas connection with instruments, not a guess. When we are done, the furnace has proven itself safe and correct through a full cycle, and if it is nearing the end of its useful life, we tell you honestly.

How to Prevent Furnace Repair Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
  • If you have a high-efficiency furnace, check that the condensate drain is flowing freely
  • Book a fall tune-up so weak igniters and fouled sensors are caught before the first freeze
  • Put a carbon monoxide detector on every level and test them monthly
  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
