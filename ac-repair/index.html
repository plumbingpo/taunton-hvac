When the air conditioning quits in Taunton, MA, the house tells you fast, and so does everyone in it. The most common failure we see is the humble run capacitor: a few dollars of electronics that starts the compressor and fan, weakens quietly for years, and finally lets go on a brutal afternoon, leaving the outdoor unit humming but the fan still. Right behind it come pitted contactors that no longer pass power cleanly, condenser fan motors with dry bearings, refrigerant leaks that slowly bleed the system's capacity away, and evaporator coils that ice over solid whenever airflow collapses behind a clogged filter.

Warning Signs for AC Repair

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • Bubbling or hissing noises that point to a possible refrigerant leak
  • Room-temperature air from the registers even though the AC is running
  • Higher summer bills even though the house still feels warm and humid
  • An iced-over evaporator coil or frozen refrigerant lines
  • The outdoor unit humming or clicking but the fan not spinning
  • A burning smell or repeated breaker trips when the AC starts
  • An AC that turns on and off every few minutes but never cools down the house
  • Water dripping from the indoor unit or showing up on the ceiling below

Why Professional AC Repair Matters

Start with the refrigerant. It lives in a sealed, pressurized loop, venting it is prohibited under federal rules, and correct service requires gauges, a vacuum pump, and a recovery machine rather than a store-bought recharge kit. Add capacitors that hold a dangerous charge with the power off and a condensing unit fed by 240 volts, and the risks add up quickly. Then there are the diagnostic traps: an iced coil often points to poor airflow rather than low charge, and topping off a system with an unrepaired leak lets moisture and acid slowly damage the compressor while your money leaks out with the refrigerant.

A professional repair looks at the whole system, not just the symptom. Our technicians meter electrical parts before replacing them, make charge decisions from measured superheat and subcooling, locate leaks with electronic detectors and dye, and check the filter, blower, and static pressure any time airflow could be the issue. The result is a repair that lasts the season instead of a guess that fails in three weeks. If your system is not worth saving, we will tell you directly, backed by numbers rather than a sales pitch.

How to Prevent AC Repair Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Rinse the outdoor condenser coil each spring and keep vegetation two feet back
  • Pay attention to buzzing or clicking when the AC starts, a common warning of a failing capacitor
  • Change the filter monthly during cooling season; starved airflow is how coils freeze
  • Leave vents open in every room, since closing them strains the blower
  • Never enclose the condenser; it needs open space on every side to shed heat
  • Clear the condensate drain when you first turn on the AC each year
  • Have the AC serviced every spring, ahead of the first real heat
